# Health Insurance for International Students

※ The insurance policies listed below are required only if you are residing in Korea. If you are not staying in Korea, enrollment is not required. However, for National Health Insurance, students who leave Korea for a period of less than one month are still required to pay the insurance premium (premium payment is exempt only for absences of one month or longer).

※ For detailed information on National Health Insurance, please contact 1577-1000. For detailed information on International Student Insurance (University-affiliated provider: DB Insurance), please refer to the following website: http://n.foreignerdb.com/konkuk1

## 1. National Health Insurance (NHI)

A. Covers approximately 50% of medical expenses.

B. Does not cover actual out-of-pocket medical expenses (This is why enrollment in private medical insurance for international students is recommended.)

C. Enrollment is mandatory.

D. Monthly premiums vary by individual, generally ranging from KRW 50,000 to KRW 150,000.

## 2. Mandatory Application of National Health Insurance (Effective March 1, 2021)

A. Eligibility Acquisition Date: Enrollment is automatically applied based on the eligibility start date as outlined below.

1. Study Abroad (D-2 Visa)

   - First entry into Korea: Date of Alien Registration
   - Re-entry into Korea: Date of re-entry

B. General Training (D-4 Visa), including Korean language programs:

   - Six (6) months after the date of entry into Korea

C. Overseas Koreans and Korean Nationals Residing Abroad (F-4 Visa) enrolled as students:

   - Date of admission to the university after entry into Korea

## 3. How to Apply for National Health Insurance (Information on Mandatory Enrollment for International Students)

- For detailed instructions on applying for National Health Insurance, please refer to the following link: https://www.nhis.or.kr/nhis/together/wbhaea01000m01.do?mode=view&articleNo=10804491

※ Students who are Japanese or French nationals may apply for an exemption from mandatory enrollment with the National Health Insurance Service if they are already covered by their home country's health insurance. Exemptions are granted after review and approval by the National Health Insurance Service.

## 4. How to Pay Insurance Premiums

A. Insurance premium bills are mailed monthly to the address registered by the student (e.g., address reported to the community service center or the Immigration Office). If you wish to receive the bill by email, you must apply separately.

→ Application methods:

- Visit the National Health Insurance Service (NHIS) office
- By phone: Korean: 1577-1000 / English, Chinese, Vietnamese, Uzbek: 033-811-2000
- By email: 0100390@nhis.or.kr

B. Premiums must be paid by the 25th of each month.

C. Payment Methods

① Automatic Bank Transfer → Apply by visiting the NHIS office, by phone (numbers listed above), or by email (0100390@nhis.or.kr)

② Payment Using Standard OCR Bill → Pay in person at a bank

③ Payment Using QR Code on the Bill → Pay at a convenience store → Or scan the QR code via KakaoTalk to pay using KakaoPay Money

④ Virtual Account Transfer → Transfer to the virtual account number indicated on the bill

## 5. Important Notes

A. Failure to enroll in the National Health Insurance (NHI) may result in disadvantages when applying to the Ministry of Justice for visa extensions or other types of stay authorization. Please refer to the details below for additional consequences.

① Restriction of Insurance Benefits
If insurance premiums are not paid by the due date (the 25th of each month), health insurance benefits will be restricted starting from the 1st day of the following month until all outstanding premiums are fully paid. During this period, coverage at hospitals and clinics will be limited.

② Restrictions on Visa Extension and Stay Authorization
When applying to the Ministry of Justice for visa extensions or other residence permits, disadvantages such as restrictions on the extension of stay may apply.

- This applies when unpaid health insurance premiums exceed KRW 500,000, or when other outstanding charges exceed KRW 100,000.

③ Compulsory Collection for Delinquency
If insurance premiums remain unpaid after a formal notice and the specified deadline, compulsory collection procedures may be initiated, including seizure of property, vehicles, or bank deposits.

B. National Health Insurance premiums are exempt for periods during which a student stays outside Korea for one month or longer.
